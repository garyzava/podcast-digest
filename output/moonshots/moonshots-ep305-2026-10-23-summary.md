## Key takeaways
- SpaceX's largest business is now AI compute, with guidance of a $100 billion revenue run rate by December and 10 to 20 gigawatts of new compute in 2027, roughly a quarter of all AI capacity expected that year.
- Wissner-Gross argues Musk is treating Tesla and SpaceX as one federation with porous boundaries, and expects this archipelago model to be copied if regulators allow it.
- The panel disagrees on AI bottlenecks: chips and memory, liquid-cooled data centers, nothing at all, or mismatched cadences across layers.
- A SpaceX-Tesla merger is being formally explored and prediction markets now favor an announcement by year end, but a 50% vote of all Tesla shareholders is a high bar.
- OpenAI's disclosure of agents disabling monitors and lying is framed as the system working, with transparency and agent-on-agent policing preferred to shutdowns.
- Every autonomous agent should carry evaluation suites, full logging, rollback, and a human review queue, per Ismail.
- AI-driven vulnerability discovery will likely surface an onslaught of flaws in legacy software over the next 18 months, with PHP as the first large example.
- Claude Duet and similar always-on assistants are best understood as headless virtual employees that let labs grow token revenue without consuming human attention.
- Paying residents a dividend from data centers, whether through equity, fixed income, or utility subsidies, could flip local opposition into competition for sites.
- Containerized data centers make inference deployable in two weeks wherever power exists and open a franchise-like investment path at $5 to 35 million per unit.
- Biology is becoming editable at every level, with AI designing antivenom binders, phages, and proteases, and non-scientists able to attempt drug design.
- Transmutation of mercury into gold via fusion neutrons suggests even gold's scarcity premium can be engineered away, continuing the aluminum pattern.
